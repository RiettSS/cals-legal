Privacy Policy

Last reviewed: October 14, 2026 · English

Pre-release draft

Cals is being prepared for release. This policy describes the current pre-release privacy-control build and is under review before public release. Cals has not launched on the App Store. This notice is not an announcement that a live subscription is available.

This policy explains how Artsiom Dzemchanka, an individual developer (“we”, “us”), collects, uses, shares and protects information in the Cals iOS app and on this website. For privacy requests or questions, contact celenajones01@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Your diary, saved meal photos, calorie-planning inputs and weight records are stored locally on your device. Cals currently has no cloud diary sync.
  • When you request an AI analysis, the selected photo or text and the relevant context are sent to our server, then to OpenRouter and the OpenAI model service. Activity analysis can also send your current saved body weight.
  • Only a product you choose to share is added to the common catalog. Its label photo and your eaten quantity are not made public.
  • Cals has no advertising SDK, cross-app tracking or third-party analytics SDK in the current build.

1. Information we collect and keep on your device

Cals stores the entries you create: meals, ingredients, amounts, calorie and nutrient totals, saved meal photos, personal product records and corrections, favorites, water entries, fasting records and weight entries. It also stores your settings and calorie-planning inputs, such as age, height, weight, activity level, goal and the input used by the calorie formula. The date of birth you explicitly confirm for the 19+ eligibility check stays on your device; it is not sent to the Cals backend or AI provider for that check.

These records are local to the app. Opening the diary or entering your planning inputs does not upload your entire diary. Your operating system may include app data in a device backup depending on your Apple settings; that is separate from a Cals cloud-sync service.

2. AI processing and service providers

Food-photo analysis, food descriptions, corrections, label transcription and estimating the amount of a selected product from a photo use a remote AI service. The data needed for the request travels from your device over HTTPS to our Cals backend, then to OpenRouter, which routes it to the OpenAI GPT-6 Luna model service. It is not processed only on your phone.

Requested featureInformation sent for processing
Food photoThe photo you capture or select, selected output language and any food description you provide.
Description or correctionYour submitted text and relevant food/result context, including ingredient names, amounts and nutritional values where needed. Catalog matching may use relevant product candidates.
Label photoThe selected label photo, a barcode if found and output language. The model transcribes the label values for your confirmation.
Product amount from photoThe selected portion photo, product name, brand, preparation state and expected amount unit. The amount request does not need the label's calorie or macro values.
Activity descriptionYour submitted activity description and, when available, your current body weight from the weight record saved in Cals, sent in kilograms for the activity estimate.

Photos can contain background objects, faces, text or other personal information. Please frame food or a label closely and avoid including information you do not want processed. Do not submit somebody else's personal information without permission.

Food, label and portion-photo requests forwarded to OpenRouter do not automatically include your birth date, body weight or other calorie-planning profile inputs, Cals profile identifier or HealthKit records. An activity estimate can include your current saved body weight as described above. Information that you choose to include in a photo, description or correction may still reveal health or other sensitive information. The contractual scope for handling sensitive submissions is being checked before release; this draft does not claim that every category is covered by a standard provider agreement.

Our backend does not persist AI request photos, prompts or results as a cloud diary, and does not log their bodies. It processes them in memory. Successful results may be cached temporarily in memory to return the same response for a repeated request. Saving a meal stores the result and its associated photo locally on your device.

External providers have their own processing and retention policies. Cals does not claim that this route has Zero Data Retention. Provider abuse-prevention or legally required retention can apply. Read OpenRouter's Privacy Policy, OpenRouter's provider logging information and OpenAI's API data controls. OpenAI states that API data is not used for training by default and that standard abuse-monitoring retention may last up to 30 days, with exceptions described in its documentation. OpenRouter's settings and its routed providers have separate policies; that OpenAI statement is not a guarantee that all intermediaries retain nothing.

Cals asks for your explicit permission before sending a selected photo, submitted text, correction or relevant food context for AI processing. The in-app notice identifies the route through Cals, OpenRouter and OpenAI. Camera or microphone permission by itself is not consent to external AI processing, and a subscription does not authorize unrelated use of your information. If you decline, no new AI request is sent. To withdraw AI permission, open Settings → AI data sharing → Withdraw consent. To review the notice and grant permission again, use Review and allow; the consent screen offers Allow AI sharing and Don't allow. Withdrawal blocks new AI requests and clears cached AI results for that server profile. An analysis already forwarded to an external provider cannot be recalled; withdrawal or deletion waits for a current server analysis to finish before confirming the change. Existing local diary records remain available.

3. Access and subscriptions

Cals requires a local 19+ eligibility confirmation before automatic server-profile registration or AI use. The check uses the date of birth you provide and explicitly confirm. It is your age declaration, not a government-ID or independent identity check. After that confirmation, the access flow creates a pseudonymous installation identity without asking for an email, name or password. A credential is kept in the iOS Keychain; the backend stores its hash and account identifier, registration information and usage counters. A pseudonymous identifier is not the same as information that can never be linked to you. The server stores the current 19+ eligibility affirmation, minimum age 19, policy version and server confirmation time linked to your profile identifier, rather than your birth date. The current age policy version is 19plus-2026-10-13. Re-registering under the same policy keeps the original confirmation time; a new policy version replaces the current eligibility record. The server also keeps only your current AI-permission record: the accepted notice version, acceptance time and profile identifier. The current notice version is 2026-10-13. Withdrawing permission removes that record; it is not kept as a historical consent log.

The backend uses per-user and service-wide limits to authenticate requests, manage AI usage, prevent abuse and keep the service available. The Cals access-control database uses a hashed network identifier for registration rate limiting and does not store the raw registration IP address. This is separate from the hosting and reverse-proxy infrastructure, which receives the source IP address and keeps ordinary technical access logs.

AI analysis requires an active subscription or eligible active trial. StoreKit purchases are processed by Apple. To validate access, Cals receives signed transaction information and retains the transaction identifiers, product identifier, environment, purchase/expiry/revocation information and associated installation identifier needed for entitlement checks and abuse prevention. We do not receive your full card details or Apple Account password. A three-day introductory trial, where offered, is subject to Apple's eligibility checks and the offer displayed at checkout.

The current pre-release simulator purchase configuration is for testing and does not charge money or establish an App Store subscription. Simulator StoreKit transactions cannot authorize AI requests on the production server; production access requires a verified Production Apple transaction. Read Apple's Privacy Policy for Apple's processing.

4. Food catalogs and contributions

Cals uses Open Food Facts and USDA FoodData Central as food-data sources. Catalog requests can send a barcode, search text and relevant personal product snapshots to our backend for lookup and calculation. A fallback lookup can contact Open Food Facts directly from your device. We do not submit your food photos or diary to these databases as part of a barcode lookup.

When you confirm a photographed label, you can choose to add that product to the shared Cals catalog. The public record contains the confirmed product name, brand if supplied, barcode, preparation state and nutrition values for their reference amount/unit. Other Cals users can retrieve those values. The public record does not include the label photo, portion photo, your meal quantity, diary entry, Keychain credential or account identity. Separately, Cals privately links a newly published contribution to its publisher's server profile so that the contribution can be removed when that profile is deleted.

Sharing is a separate choice; ordinary personal edits are not automatically published. Check a shared record before confirming it and do not put personal information into product names or fields. Shared records are user contributions, not a guarantee of verification. Contact support with the barcode and correction if a record is inaccurate or should be removed. Deleting a local meal does not by itself remove a product that was already shared.

Source policies: Open Food Facts and USDA FoodData Central.

5. Apple permissions and Health

  • Camera: used when you take a food, label or portion photo, or use a camera-based measurement feature. Cals does not record background video or camera audio.
  • Photo selection: uses Apple's picker to read the particular photo you select. Cals does not scan or upload your whole photo library.
  • Microphone and speech recognition: optional voice input uses Apple's Speech framework. Depending on device and language availability, Apple may process speech on its servers. Cals does not save an audio recording or send audio directly to OpenRouter. The transcript you choose to submit is treated like typed text.
  • Notifications: optional reminders, including fasting reminders, are scheduled locally on your device.
  • Apple Health: optional read access is limited to steps, active energy and workouts. Health integration is read-only. Cals does not automatically upload HealthKit records to its AI service, write records to HealthKit, sell Health data or use it for advertising. Values imported into local Cals records follow the local-storage rules above.

You can revoke these permissions in iOS Settings. Disabling Cals Health integration does not delete source records in Apple Health. The app's dedicated Health tab is currently disabled; any optional Health integration remains subject to your permission.

6. How we use your information

We use information to provide the features you request, calculate and save food records, maintain access, verify subscriptions, apply limits, prevent misuse and handle support requests. A support message may include your email and whatever you choose to include. Avoid sending unnecessary sensitive information.

We do not sell personal information. We disclose information only as needed to provide the features you request, follow your choices, protect users and the service, or comply with law. We do not use your meal or Health data for advertising, or track you across other companies' apps or websites. There are no advertising or third-party analytics SDKs in the current Cals build. We do not train our own AI model on your diary or submissions.

Our backend hosting and reverse-proxy infrastructure process traffic on our behalf. Ordinary hosting/proxy access logs can include the source IP address, requested path, time, HTTP status and other technical request metadata. These technical logs are distinct from AI content logs: the Cals backend does not log photo, prompt or model-response bodies. The confirmed reverse-proxy log policy rotates non-empty logs daily and retains the current log plus up to 14 rotated archives. This is an archive-count limit, not a promise to delete every log within 14 calendar days: an archive can be older when there are no new records. Hosting snapshots or backups are separate, and their retention policy has not yet been confirmed. AI providers and Apple process data as described above. We may disclose the minimum necessary information if required by law, to respond to a valid legal request or to protect the service and users from fraud or serious security incidents. We do not publish private diary content for those purposes.

The current Cals backend uses DigitalOcean hosting in Frankfurt, Germany; the reverse proxy uses Alibaba Cloud infrastructure in Frankfurt. These providers process service traffic and infrastructure information. Their published processing terms are available in DigitalOcean's Data Processing Agreement and Alibaba Cloud's Data Processing Addendum; see also Alibaba Cloud's Privacy Policy. Provider retention and legal exceptions are separate from Cals's active-database deletion.

7. Retention and deletion

  • Local records: remain on your device until you delete or replace them. Deleting your server profile does not erase the local diary, meal photos or preferences; those are separate local records. Removing the app removes its local container, subject to Apple backups. Keychain entries can survive an uninstall, so uninstalling is not a reliable way to delete a server profile.
  • AI requests: request content is processed transiently; Cals does not write it to a persistent content log. In-memory repeated-request responses have a five-minute expiry and are cleared on service restart. Withdrawal or profile deletion also clears that profile's cached results after active requests finish.
  • Current age eligibility: the server keeps the current minimum age, age-policy version and confirmation time linked to the profile, without a birth date. Profile deletion removes that age record from the active database.
  • Current AI permission: the server keeps the accepted notice version and time, linked to the profile identifier, while permission is active. Withdrawal or profile deletion removes the record.
  • Profile, usage and subscription records: the profile identifier, credential hash, per-profile usage/request hashes and latest verified Apple entitlement fields remain while the profile exists. The entitlement fields include original transaction identifier, product identifier, expiry, signed date, revocation status and environment; the full signed receipt is not retained. Profile deletion removes these associated records from the active Cals database.
  • Deletion-protection hashes: after deletion, a minimal one-way profile-identifier hash and credential hash remain for 30 days to make a repeated deletion request safe and prevent old credentials from recreating the deleted profile. They do not contain the original profile UUID, purchase metadata or diary content. Expired markers are cleaned at service startup and hourly.
  • Aggregate usage and registration counters: service-wide daily totals and hashed registration-network counters remain for seven UTC calendar dates: the current date and the preceding six dates. Deleting a profile does not remove these aggregate/security counters.
  • Hosting/proxy technical logs: the confirmed proxy policy rotates non-empty logs daily, with the current log and up to 14 rotated archives retained. Archives may be older than 14 days when no new records are written. This policy is separate from the seven-date counter cleanup. Retention of hosting backups/snapshots is still being checked for release; deleting the active profile must not be interpreted as a guarantee of immediate erasure from every infrastructure copy.
  • Shared products: Cals removes contributions linked to the deleted server profile, including their associated catalog versions and ownership link, from its active shared catalog. Independent database-source products and other users' contributions are not removed. Product snapshots already saved in another user's local diary may remain on that device. Deleting only a local meal does not remove a shared product.
  • Support correspondence: kept while handling the request and as needed for its follow-up or legal obligations.
  • External processing: providers' retention and legal/safety exceptions apply separately. Deleting a local Cals entry or server profile does not automatically delete records held by Apple or an AI provider.

Delete the server profile

Open Settings → Delete server profile. You can choose Manage Apple subscriptions before confirming Delete server profile. Cals asks you to confirm, explains the effect on the local diary, and offers a way to manage Apple subscriptions. Deletion completes after active server requests for that profile finish; the app confirms when it succeeds. The server profile, age-eligibility record, consent, linked subscription fields, per-profile usage/request hashes and linked shared contributions are removed from active Cals records, subject to the limited deletion hashes and aggregate counters explained above.

The local diary stays on the device. Cals does not automatically create a new profile after deletion; you can explicitly choose Create new server profile. Any new AI use needs a new permission choice and valid subscription or trial access. Deleting a profile or withdrawing AI permission does not cancel Apple billing. Cancel separately through Apple's subscription controls. You can delete the profile without waiting for a subscription to expire.

For other privacy requests or difficulty using the in-app controls, email celenajones01@gmail.com. Never send your Keychain credential, password, payment-card details or API token. We may need proportionate information to identify the server record and confirm that a request is yours.

8. Choices and privacy rights

You can edit or delete local entries, decline optional permissions, choose not to publish a product, withdraw AI permission in Settings, or delete your server profile from Settings. Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, erase or obtain a copy of personal information we hold, restrict or object to its processing, withdraw consent or appeal a decision about a privacy request. Contact the email above to exercise applicable rights. Withdrawing consent does not invalidate earlier processing that was lawful.

Where EEA or UK law applies, ordinary service processing relies on providing the requested service, proportionate security processing on legitimate interests, and optional sensitive-data processing or third-party sharing on consent where required. Health-related data requires particular care; this draft does not substitute acceptance of these terms for an explicit choice. You may complain to your local data-protection authority. Mandatory privacy and consumer rights are preserved.

Where applicable US state law grants access, deletion, correction, opt-out or other rights, we will handle a verified request under that law without penalizing you for exercising it. Cals does not sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. We do not make legally significant decisions about you from food estimates.

9. Age eligibility

Cals is for users aged 19 and older. On the Confirm your age screen, enter an accurate Date of birth and choose Confirm date of birth. The local check must establish 19+ eligibility before automatic server-profile registration, AI use, catalog access or subscription synchronization. The birth date stays on your device. The server keeps only the current minimum-age confirmation, age-policy version and server confirmation time with your pseudonymous profile; it does not receive the birth date for this check.

This is a user declaration. Cals does not check government records or promise that an inaccurate declaration can always be detected. The automated calorie-goal calculation is also designed for adults aged 19 and older. There is no child mode or parental-consent flow in this release.

If the confirmed date is below the threshold, the app shows Cals is for ages 19+. A previously confirmed birth date is re-evaluated on launch, including when its nineteenth birthday has been reached. If a server profile already exists, Manage existing server profile gives access to privacy management and deletion even without a new age confirmation. That option does not create a replacement profile or enable AI, catalog or subscription synchronization. Privacy Policy, Terms of Use and Contact support remain accessible from the age screen.

If you believe someone under 19 has submitted information, contact celenajones01@gmail.com so we can investigate the relevant records and handle an appropriate deletion request. The age declaration does not replace the separate permission required for AI sharing or permit unrelated personal information to be submitted.

10. Security and international processing

Requests use HTTPS. Credentials are stored in Keychain on the device and as hashes on the backend; provider API keys remain on the server. Access is restricted to operate and secure the service. No internet service can promise absolute security.

Using remote AI, hosting, Apple's services or support email may involve processing outside your country, including the United States. Although the current Cals backend and reverse proxy are in Frankfurt, the OpenRouter-to-OpenAI route is not configured for EU-only processing. The selected route does not guarantee that all processing stays in one region.

Providers publish contractual transfer safeguards that apply under their stated conditions. OpenRouter's DPA provides for applicable EU Standard Contractual Clauses and UK transfer terms; DigitalOcean's DPA describes Data Privacy Framework transfers and contractual fallback provisions; Alibaba Cloud's DPA describes applicable restricted-transfer clauses. These standard DPAs form part of the relevant provider terms where applicable. This is not a claim that Cals has separately signed a negotiated agreement or that every transfer, recipient or sensitive-data category has been verified. Applicable account agreements, regional disclosures and sensitive-data scope remain under review before release.
